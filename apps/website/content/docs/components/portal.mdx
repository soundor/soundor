`Portal` renders its children elsewhere in the plugin view while they stay
in their component's React tree: their context, state and effects are kept.
It is a Soundor extension, not a React Native component, built on the
renderer's own portals: no ReactDOM, and no other window.

## Over everything

`<Portal>` (also `host={null}`) renders into the view's overlay layer:

- above all of the content, whatever its `zIndex`, and out of any `overflow`
  clipping or scrolling;
- in surface coordinates: place it with an event's `pageX`/`pageY` or a
  node's `getBoundingClientRect()` ([coordinates](/docs/components/events#coordinates));
- filling the view, letting the pointer through where it draws nothing;
- above the portals opened before it, so one opened from inside another (a
  dropdown in a dialog) is on top of it.

There is one overlay layer, not one per kind of overlay, and it is not
exported: `<Portal>` is the way in. A context menu is `onContextMenu`, the
pointer's position and a portal:

## Into a host

`<Portal host={host}>` renders into the `<Portal.Host host={host}>`, as its
children: clipped, stacked and scrolled wherever that host is. Hosts are
opaque handles from `createPortalHost()`.

```tsx
const panelHost = createPortalHost();

<View style={{ overflow: 'hidden' }}>
  <Portal.Host
    host={panelHost}
    style={{ position: 'absolute', inset: 0, pointerEvents: 'box-none' }}
  />
</View>

<Portal host={panelHost}>…</Portal>
```

Content waits, rendering nothing, until its host mounts, goes when the host
unmounts, and comes back with it. Showing one host in two places at once is
an error.

## Events and focus

Pointer and key events bubble through the nodes as they are shown: from a
portal's content up to its host (or the overlay layer), not through the
component that declared it. Focus follows React: content a `FocusScope`
renders through a portal belongs to that scope.
