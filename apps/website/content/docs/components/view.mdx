A `View` is a box: it lays its children out with flexbox, paints a
background and a border, clips, and receives events. It is a column by
default, as in React Native.

<Snippet name="view" height={230} />

- **Layout:** `flexDirection`, `justifyContent`, `alignItems`, `gap`,
  `flex`, sizes, margins and padding: see [Style](/docs/components/style).
- **Focus:** `focusable` makes it take keyboard focus when pressed or tabbed
  to; then it receives key events ([Events](/docs/components/events)).
- **To assistive technology** a view is not an element itself; what it
  contains is, unless it is given a role or `accessible`
  ([Accessibility](/docs/components/accessibility)).

## Scrolling

Set `overflowY: 'scroll'` on a view to scroll its children vertically,
`overflowX: 'scroll'` for horizontal scrolling, or `overflow: 'scroll'`
for both. It clips its content and scrolls by wheel or trackpad, by its
scrollbar, or from code. `Animated.View` scrolls with the same styles.

<Snippet name="scrolling" height={230} />

- **Size:** give the scrolling view a height or a `flex` that gives it one.
  Style its children directly; when the content needs a box of its own,
  put it in another `View` inside.
- **Axes:** `overflowX` and `overflowY` win over `overflow`, whatever the
  order. Set an axis to `'hidden'` to clip without scrolling it.
  See [Overflow](/docs/components/style#overflow).
- **`onScroll`** fires as it scrolls, by any means; read the node's `scrollTop`
  and `scrollLeft`. Scrolled from code, it fires once on the next frame,
  rather than during the call.
- **Accessibility:** a scrolling view is not an element itself; what it
  contains is. Given a label or `accessible`, it is exposed as a scroll view
  unless given another role.
- **Indicators** are drawn over the content, taking no space. A mouse near
  the edge shows a track under a grown bar: the bar drags, and a press on the
  track jumps there. That 12 pixel strip takes the mouse from what is under
  it, so leave room with padding if something important is at that edge.
- **`scrollIndicators={false}`** leaves the indicators out. It scrolls the
  same; for a scrollbar of your own, see
  [`useScrollbar`](/docs/hooks/use-scrollbar).

### Scrolling from code

The node, from `ref`, scrolls as an element does on the Web:

```tsx
list.current?.scrollTo({ top: 0, behavior: 'smooth' });
list.current?.scrollBy({ top: 100 });
row.current?.scrollIntoView({ block: 'nearest', behavior: 'smooth' });
```

- **`scrollTo()` and `scrollBy()`** take `{ top, left, behavior }`, or
  `(left, top)`. They move the axes the style scrolls, clamped to the content,
  and nothing on a node that does not scroll.
- **`behavior: 'smooth'`** takes 300 ms, eased in and out, the same in both
  runtimes, a `scroll` event each frame. A wheel, a scrollbar, or scrolling the
  node again stops it where it is. With reduced motion asked for, it is there at
  once. `'auto'` (the default) and `'instant'` jump.
- **`scrollIntoView()`** scrolls every view around the node that scrolls,
  innermost first, to show it: `block` vertically and `inline` horizontally,
  each `'start'`, `'center'`, `'end'` or `'nearest'` (no more than it takes).
  It defaults to `block: 'start'`, `inline: 'nearest'`; `false` puts it at the
  end. It goes by layout boxes, so a transform does not change where it scrolls
  to.
