---
title: useScrollbar
description: A scrollbar of your own, drawn however you like.
---

`useScrollbar` gives a track and a thumb of your own what a scrollbar does: the
thumb shows what is in view and drags, and a press on the track jumps there.
They are ordinary nodes, so they look like whatever you draw, wherever you put
them, in every runtime.

```tsx
const scroll = useRef<UiNode>(null);
const bar = useScrollbar(scroll);

return (
  <View style={{ flex: 1 }}>
    <ScrollView ref={scroll} scrollIndicators={false} style={{ flex: 1 }}>
      {children}
    </ScrollView>
    {bar.visible && (
      <View {...bar.trackProps} style={styles.track}>
        <View
          {...bar.thumbProps}
          style={[styles.thumb, bar.pressed && styles.thumbPressed]}
        />
      </View>
    )}
  </View>
);
```

- **Where it goes:** beside the `ScrollView`, not in it, where it would scroll
  away. Position it absolutely to draw over the content, as the built-in
  indicators do, or give it its own room in a row. To keep that room when there
  is nothing to scroll, render the track always and only the thumb by
  `bar.visible`.
- **The thumb** is the track's first child, at its start. The hook sets its
  length and its `transform` without rendering, so leave those out of its style.
- **`axis`** is `'vertical'` by default; a second call with `'horizontal'` gives
  the other bar. **`minThumbLength`** is 16 by default.
- **`visible`, `hovered` and `pressed`** render when they change, and scrolling
  does not.
- **Cost:** it reads the scroll view's sizes each frame while mounted, as its
  content can change size with no event to say so.
