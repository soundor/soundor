---
title: useScrollbar
description: A scrollbar of your own, drawn however you like.
---

`useScrollbar` gives tracks and thumbs of your own what scrollbars do: a
thumb shows what is in view and drags, and a press on its track jumps there.
They are ordinary nodes, so they look like whatever you draw, wherever you put
them, in every runtime. They scroll any view whose style scrolls: a
`ScrollView`, or a `View` or `Animated.View` with `overflow: 'scroll'`.

```tsx
const list = useRef<UiNode>(null);
const { vertical } = useScrollbar(list);

return (
  <View style={{ flex: 1 }}>
    <View
      ref={list}
      scrollIndicators={false}
      style={{ flex: 1, overflowY: 'scroll' }}
    >
      {children}
    </View>
    {vertical.visible && (
      <View {...vertical.trackProps} style={styles.track}>
        <View
          {...vertical.thumbProps}
          style={[styles.thumb, vertical.pressed && styles.thumbPressed]}
        />
      </View>
    )}
  </View>
);
```

- **Both axes:** it returns `{ vertical, horizontal }`, a bar for each; draw
  the ones you need. Each has its own `visible`, `hovered`, `pressed`,
  `trackProps` and `thumbProps`. Where both show, each `visible` says whether to
  leave the corner to the other.
- **Where they go:** beside the view, not in it, where they would scroll
  away. Position them absolutely to draw over the content, as the built-in
  indicators do, or give them their own room in a row. To keep that room when
  there is nothing to scroll, render the track always and only the thumb by
  `visible`.
- **The thumb** is the track's first child, at its start. The hook sets its
  length and its `transform` without rendering, so leave those out of its style.
- **`visible`** is true where the view's style scrolls that axis and its
  content is longer than it. **`minThumbLength`** is 16 by default.
- **The nodes** are yours to reach too: `trackProps.ref.current`, to animate a
  track say.
- **`visible`, `hovered` and `pressed`** render when they change, and scrolling
  does not.
- **Cost:** none while nothing changes. A `ResizeObserver` tells it when the
  view, its children or a track change size, and its `scroll` events tell it
  of scrolling.
